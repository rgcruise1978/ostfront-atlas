# Phase V maps — research, coverage and release review

## Scope and preservation

Eight dated regional reconstructions, 15 November–5 December 1941, use the established Poland / Barbarossa I–IV MapLibre engine and palette. They contain 901 dated military, logistics, control, front and change records. All 50 article JSON files, the shared palette, the earlier 41 snapshot records and earlier historical feature records are preserved. This is military map implementation, not a new editorial revision of the studies.

The before-state is `before-state.zip`, with SHA-256 evidence in `before-manifest.json`. `coverage-matrix.json` accounts for all 281 substantive paragraphs, including historical asides. Every paragraph is accessible in Study Context, with article section, citations and qualified feature associations. Spatial associations do not certify every assertion in a paragraph as precisely mapped. Diplomatic discussion, broad statistics, unlocated testimony and later consequences remain contextual. `date-summary.json` and `validation.json` provide the feature inventory by date.

### Retained evidence from the published corpus and prior map review

All eight published studies were read in full for this task. Their citation locators and earlier evidence/access qualifications are retained. The prior editorial audit in `phase-v-edit-review/evidence-audit.json` distinguishes inspected passages, public academic extracts, transcribed records and catalogue-only material. Prior Phase IV research supplies the Transnistria regional polygon, stable Finnish reach, inherited physical geography, Hoth's 5 October transfer, Tikhvin context and institutional civilian sources. These were reused with their recorded qualifications; this task does not claim a fresh independent reading of every complete book or every inherited source.

Stahel public extracts are not full-book access. Catalogue descriptions of orders do not prove that an archival image was read. Multiple reproductions of one document do not count as independent confirmation. The Library of Congress daily situation-map candidates were unavailable in the prior source audit; no new access or examination is claimed and they are not the basis for exact daily geometry. Failed API attempts are not evidence; the successful official OSM relation data are the inputs to the waterway builder.

## Chronology and consequential limits

- Each map is a qualified daily synthesis, not a uniform end-of-day operational report. Feature event intervals and precision notes govern intraday interpretation.
- **15 November:** the renewed offensive opens in stages; unexecuted northern/southern objectives are plans, normally hidden.
- **18 November:** Dedilovo and the Tula bypass are dated separately from Uzlovaya (21), Venev (24), Mikhaylov (27) and the Kashira raid/counterattack.
- **21 November:** Rostov is German-held; Soviet withdrawal across the Don and pressure on the northern flank are distinct operations.
- **24 November:** Klin and Solnechnogorsk reflect the northern advance; Istra's later capture is not backdated. The 78th Rifle Division becomes the 9th Guards only from 26 November. Belov's 1st Guards designation is also applied only after its redesignation.
- **28 November:** the Yakhroma crossing and temporary east-bank foothold are shown as an intraday contested area. The Soviet response, the later withdrawal and the order to turn south have distinct times and notes. The bridgehead is absent on 29 November. The close view includes Yakhroma, Dmitrov, Peremilovo, the canal and Yakhroma river; crossing points are approximate historical localities, not surveyed bridge coordinates.
- **29 November:** Rostov is recovered. Arrows show retreat from the city, not a completed Mius front. The 30 November–1 December withdrawal appears on 2 and 5 December. Rundstedt's replacement is not projected back onto the 29 November situation.
- **2 December:** the 258th Infantry Division penetration is shown; its 3 December withdrawal appears only on the 5 December map. Zakharov's orders remain plans. The exact modern identities of Dednevo/Fedorovka in the account are unresolved; no precise village point is invented.
- **5 December:** the Kalinin Front's 29th Army crossing and checked attack are distinguished from Western Front attacks starting on 6 December and other scheduled 7 December actions. Future movements are hidden plans. Tikhvin remains German-held; its 9 December recovery is contextual, not shown as completed.
- **Article discrepancy retained:** the concluding study describes Guderian's proposed withdrawal in Don–Shat terms, while the consulted command account uses Shat–Upa. The map identifies the disagreement and shows a proposed regional withdrawal without asserting an exact completed new line. Article prose is unchanged.
- The northern Finnish envelope is broad inherited regional context, not a newly established daily tactical line in Karelia. Romanian participation is a qualified contingent label; later December formations are not backdated. Unestablished commanders are left unnamed.
- Front joins, formation sector markers, road/rail corridors and occupation envelopes are generalized. They are not precise headquarters, surveyed front lines, secure control of every village, verified railway capacity, exact railheads or independently observed daily reports for every sector. Roads and railways show documented corridors, with serviceability explicitly qualified.
- Civilian and prisoner accounts use documented town/region associations. No witness is assigned an invented street, camp or home. Refugee flows, diplomatic exchanges and later outcomes without usable spatial evidence remain accessible context.

## Validation and presentation

`validate-phase-five-maps.py` checks source/locator membership, unique IDs, geometry, article/context links, date membership, future-action exclusion, formation renaming, city control, full German shading and preservation. The legacy geography validator now accepts all 49 dates; its article baseline for this release is the saved pre-task manifest rather than an obsolete Phase III editorial hash set. Earlier map gates remain in place. Build, metadata, data and Phase IV regression checks are recorded alongside the review.

Browser evidence covers all eight dates at desktop/mobile widths, additional canal/Don/Tikhvin/Crimea/Tula/Europe views, contextual links and sources, presets, exports and representative Poland / Phases I–IV maps. A development-server screenshot run was interrupted by hot reload; the final review uses the static production build. Technical checks establish coherent behaviour and preserved files, not surveyed historical precision.

## Reproduction and release

Use the existing Python environment with Shapely for `scripts/build-phase-five-maps.py`; the records and operations modules hold dated inputs. `scripts/build-phase-five-waterways.py` reads the successful raw OSM relation files retained in this review's `research/` folder. Run the data/foundation/map validators, Vite build and metadata generation. Read `release.json` for the final GitHub/Netlify identity and public verification result; do not infer publication solely from a successful local build.

## Final geographic label correction

The final image review identified modern orientation names inherited from the base dataset. Phase V suppresses Volgodonsk and Zheleznogorsk (postwar foundations), omits the modern Dubna city label rather than guessing a 1941 settlement footprint, and uses Stalino for the Ukrainian city later renamed Donetsk. Supporting checks: [Volgodonsk City Duma history](https://volgodonskduma.ru/o-volgodonske/), [Dubna municipal history](https://naukograd-dubna.ru/about/story), [Zheleznogorsk city charter, article 3](https://base.garant.ru/21302660/5ac206a89ea76855804609cd950fcaf7/), and the indexed [Donetsk regional archive historical summary](https://dn.archives.gov.ua/prazdniki/85_let_donetskoi_oblasti.html) (indexed passage inspected; direct page unavailable). These display corrections are restricted to Phase V; earlier data remain preserved. Moscow and Klin receive priority locality labels. Map-ready state now waits for completed map rendering, preventing partial captures during data loading.

The Crimea regional review also removed an artificial coastal strip created by a polygon closure. The operational envelope now closes offshore through the Azov/Kerch/Black Sea waters, preserving the Soviet Kuban coast. Explicit coastal-land and Yeysk regression points guard this distinction.
