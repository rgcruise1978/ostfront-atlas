# Phase IV editorial and factual review — 15 October 2026

Scope: ten existing studies, 30 September–14 November 1941. All were read in date order and checked against the available full official histories, diplomatic documents, decree texts, scholarly excerpts, museum records and source-access notes. Review is an evidence-based editorial assessment, not a claim that every retrospective source agrees or that every full scholarly book was accessible.

## 1941-09-30 — 3,902 substantive words

Separated the 30 September southern opening from 2 October. Corrected the panzer-army designation locator. Added September tank-serviceability and fuel-train requirements with their actual dates, and the 2 October economic assessment as context only where appropriate.

## 1941-10-02 — 3,604 substantive words

Added General Georg Thomas’s dated economic assessment, distinguishing projected industrial losses from Soviet collapse. Replaced generic soldier discussion with an explicitly northern-theatre example; did not relocate that soldier to Army Group Centre.

## 1941-10-07 — 3,585 substantive words

Removed a faulty inference about Bryansk capture based on the arrival of a later formation. Distinguished encirclement from surrender and exposed incompatible retrospective prisoner totals. Added corps assignments and corrected printed-page locators for the rear-area murder case.

## 1941-10-13 — 3,529 substantive words

## 1941-10-16 — 3,603 substantive words

## 1941-10-19 — 3,555 substantive words

Distinguished the October Ishun offensive from the earlier Perekop fighting; retained Sevastopol as Soviet-held. Removed an uncorroborated seven-week tractor/tank restart claim. Used the Kharkiv museum’s tractor evacuation account with a clearly retrospective production date. Preserved Odessa massacre chronology.

## 1941-11-02 — 3,548 substantive words

Kept Tula and Sevastopol Soviet-held and the operational lull distinct from cessation of fighting. Replaced unsupported Tula household generalizations. Added a concrete weapons/transport interaction and the aid proposal received on 2 November, distinguished from deliveries.

## 1941-11-09 — 3,544 substantive words

Confirmed German capture on 8 November, the 9 November concentration directive, and constrained continuing Ladoga access. Removed an unsupported command-date dispute. Added Lena Mukhina’s documented earlier defensive labour without inventing a November diary entry.

## 1941-11-14 — 3,609 substantive words

Kept the 14 November Soviet action separate from German attacks beginning later. Removed questionable east-of-Serpukhov precision. Distinguished Stalin’s 6 and 7 November speeches, added the dated aid acceptance, and corrected the freeze chronology. Retained the 12 November map-sheet limitation.

## Common limits and decisions

- Repeated methodological passages were replaced with sourced historical material where it improved the narrative. The chronological chapters retain brief orientation and transitions.
- German official-history casualty claims are attributed, not treated as independently verified totals. Later Soviet transport and industrial outcomes are explicitly retrospective.
- Cambridge public summaries support only the material actually visible there. The Stavka source is a catalogue description, not a claimed reading of an inaccessible directive image. The LOC sequence does not establish a 14 November situation sheet.
- The federal archival factory-evacuation page could not be retrieved; it was removed from the edited text and replaced with accessible museum evidence. No unverified new source was left in the manuscript.
- Phase IV maps remain geographic references. No front lines, control polygons or formation coordinates were changed.
- All article body hashes, word counts and source IDs are recorded in review.json. Rendered-page and release checks are recorded separately.
