## Geographic material

`natural-earth-land.geojson` is a regional subset of the Natural Earth 1:50m land layer. `reference-places.geojson` selects 210 locations from Natural Earth 1:10m populated places, including 159 additional cities and towns in surrounding areas. Some labels use checked period names, with modern equivalents in the point details; the newly added labels are contemporary geographic references and are not presented as official 1939 names or population ranks. These markers do not establish jurisdiction. `major-rivers.geojson` contains nine named courses, `secondary-rivers.geojson` four local courses, and `surrounding-rivers.geojson` 33 additional named regional rivers in 51 line segments from Natural Earth river datasets. The regional rivers use line-following labels and appear progressively as visitors zoom in. River courses show generalized physical geography, not verified 1939 crossings, battle barriers, or exact historic channels. All data is served locally without third-party map tiles.

`poland-1939-reference.geojson` contains Poland's polygon from the University of Minnesota's annual 1939 historical boundary layer. `surrounding-1939-reference.geojson` selects Germany, the Soviet Union, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Romania, and Hungary from that layer. The source is a generalized, smoothed annual reconstruction without an exact day. These outlines remain fixed across the timeline and do not depict later occupation. Its Czechoslovakia polygon is excluded because it fails to represent the March 1939 formation of Slovakia and the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia. Those areas are marked by approximate text labels only; East Prussia and the Danzig area are also labels without their own border geometry. The Danzig label is geographic and does not imply that Free City status continued after German annexation.

`poland-1939.geojson` contains a dashed, date-filtered **approximate** German–Soviet partition trace starting 28 September 1939. Its route follows waypoints in a contemporary U.S. diplomatic report; the long Bug and San sections use generalized Natural Earth river coordinates. Connectors are schematic, not surveyed border coordinates. The original treaty map is linked for comparison, not reproduced. This layer does not show troop positions or battle crossings.

`control-areas.geojson` splits the fixed Poland polygon along that generalized treaty trace to depict **broad occupied sectors on 26 October only**. It was generated by `scripts/build-control-areas.mjs`. The sectors identify military occupation, not recognized sovereignty, legal annexation, exact control limits, or internal administration. The western sector includes both German-annexation claims and the General Government; these are distinguished in the feature explanation rather than presented as separate polygons. The Soviet sector is shown as occupied, without backdating later Soviet incorporation. No control fill is shown for September, because a dated and sufficiently precise front/control polygon has not been verified.

`advance-axes.geojson` contains four German and two Soviet schematic operational directions, tied to selected dates. They are vector arrows with a separate toggle and clickable source notes. The U.S. Army study and USHMM support the German north/west/south directions; the Polish IPN and USHMM support Soviet invasion from the east. Waypoints and endpoints are diagrammatic, not unit tracks, advance limits, or daily front lines. Soviet arrows do not appear before 17 September.

Historic transport evidence was reviewed in the [1939 German overview map catalogued by Gesher Galicia](https://maps.geshergalicia.org/galicia/poland-interwar-1939-2/) and [1939 railway map record at the Polish archival map index](https://igrek.amzp.pl/details.php?id=1760484). Their scanned images have not been converted to licensed, verified vectors. Roads, railways, bridges, and crossing locations are therefore still absent. Terrain, 1939 woodland/wetland extent, Polish voivodeships, and precise Danzig and southern political borders also need suitable source geometry and verification.

The fourth date is **26 October 1939** because the USHMM chronology and its article on German administration identify that date for German annexations and the establishment of the General Government civilian administration. It denotes an administrative change, unlike the two invasion dates and the treaty date.
